Hey, I'm Justin 👋

I'm a marketing analytics guy based in New York, and the question I can't stop asking is "did that ad actually cause that sale?"

My path here wasn't exactly a straight line. I studied mechanical engineering at UT Austin, spent a year checking safety margins on a nuclear test reactor (yes, really), and co-founded a boba tea vending machine startup on the side. After a stint wrangling logistics data at Goodman, I moved to New York and joined Quip, where I got to run a $1.2M geo-holdout test, and that was it for me. I was hooked on incrementality.

Since then I've been chasing that question at Magic Spoon and now Athena Club, where I work on marketing measurement: geo tests, MMTs, LTV models, and figuring out what's really driving sales across DTC and retail. Somewhere in there I also picked up a master's in analytics from Georgia Tech and built my own geo-testing tool (more on that below).

When I'm not staring at lift charts, I'm usually eating my way through New York, from tiny neighborhood spots to the place everyone's been trying to get a reservation at, or catching a live show. A good dinner followed by a concert is pretty much my perfect night.

Side project

The MMT Tool

Matched market tests are one of the best ways to measure what marketing actually does, but running one usually means a lot of R scripts and a lot of spreadsheets. So I built a tool for it.

The MMT Tool is a web app wrapped around GeoLift, Meta's open-source library for geo-based lift testing. You upload sales by zip code, and it walks you through the whole test: pick your markets, run a power analysis, lock the design, then read out the lift and iROAS by channel once the test is done.

  • Market selection and power analysis built on GeoLift's synthetic control
  • Holdout and growth tests, with spend tracked for test vs. control
  • Readouts by channel: lift, confidence intervals, and incremental ROAS
